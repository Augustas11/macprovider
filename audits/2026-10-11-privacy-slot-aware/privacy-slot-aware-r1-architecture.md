Issue #1911 capacity items for the SPEC-049 privacy class:
1. Slot-aware private provider selection. Since #1910 the reservation selectors rank fully eligible candidates with an unclaimed free seat first, then busy ones, each tier starting at a uniformly random provider. This change also counts the requesting buyer account's own undispatched reservations (an in-memory index keyed account -> provider -> binding -> expiry, bounded 4096 total / 64 per account, pruned at expiry, cleared when the chat reaches the slot wait) against a provider's free seats, so one buyer's concurrent private requests spread deterministically. The index is account-scoped so no buyer's selection reflects or can be steered by another buyer's reservations. Rotation stays a random tier start (a per-model/key-class counter was rejected in the #1910 audit for cohort concentration and because it leaks a count of other buyers' reservations).
2. Fair share for private waiters. Previously a relay-blind/privacy chat waiting on its pinned session shared the plaintext FIFO slot queue, was capped at maxPending/4 (1 by default) relay-blind waiters per provider, could not enter when plaintext filled the queue, and gave up after min(slot-queue deadline 10 s, reservation expiry). Now the per-provider queue has two FIFO lanes (plaintext; pinned), separate caps (pinned = half the plaintext cap of max(4, slots_total), at least 1), and queue grants alternate between lanes while both wait (state dropped when the queue empties). A pinned waiter waits until reservation expiry (TTL <= 30 s) while the provider keeps granting seats, and gives up as relay_blind_provider_unsupported only after one slot-queue deadline with no grant on its provider.

Design claims to check: eligibility is unchanged (ordering and waiting only); both classes are starvation-free and bounded (pinned waiter at lane position k served within 2k grants; plaintext head within 2 grants; pinned class at most half of queued grants while plaintext waits); plaintext admission, deadlines, direct dispatch and capacity shed are unchanged; no new cross-buyer timing or correlation channel. Deploy is a later Pearl runtime train; nothing here touches deploy tooling.
